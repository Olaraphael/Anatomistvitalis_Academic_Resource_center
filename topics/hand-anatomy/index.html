Hand anatomy: tendons, intrinsic muscles, nerves and grip

The hand contains eight carpal bones, five metacarpals and fourteen phalanges, along with complex tendon, ligament, nerve and vascular networks. Extrinsic flexor and extensor tendons arise in the forearm and pass through retinacula; intrinsic muscles arise and insert within the hand and refine finger positioning. The thenar muscles support thumb opposition, while hypothenar muscles act on the little finger. The median nerve supplies most thenar muscles and lateral lumbricals, while the ulnar nerve supplies most intrinsic hand muscles. Sensory territories overlap at boundaries and vary, so maps are guides rather than exact borders.

Learning objectives

  • Identify the major bony groups of the hand.
  • Distinguish extrinsic tendons from intrinsic hand muscles.
  • Summarise median and ulnar nerve motor roles.

High-yield study points

  • The carpal tunnel contains the median nerve and flexor tendons; the flexor carpi radialis tendon passes in a separate compartment.
  • The ulnar nerve and artery enter the hand through Guyon's canal, superficial to the flexor retinaculum.
  • Thumb opposition combines abduction, flexion and medial rotation at the first carpometacarpal joint.
  • The lumbricals flex metacarpophalangeal joints and extend interphalangeal joints through the extensor expansion.
  • Grip strength depends on sensation, tendon integrity, joint mobility and coordinated intrinsic-extrinsic muscle action.

Clinical connection

Median nerve compression can cause sensory symptoms and thenar weakness; ulnar nerve injury can impair finger abduction/adduction and intrinsic coordination. Clinical findings should be matched to the lesion site.

Special senses: eye, ear, smell and taste anatomy

Special senses rely on specialised receptors and neural pathways. The eye has fibrous, vascular and neural coats: sclera and cornea; choroid, ciliary body and iris; and retina. Light passes through the cornea, aqueous humour, pupil, lens and vitreous body before reaching the retina. The ear includes an external sound-collecting region, a middle ear that transmits vibrations through the ossicles, and an inner ear containing the cochlea and vestibular apparatus. Olfactory receptors occupy the superior nasal cavity; taste receptor cells are located in taste buds associated with papillae and other oral surfaces.

Learning objectives

  • Trace the passage of light through the eye.
  • Distinguish external, middle and inner ear structures.
  • Describe the receptor locations for smell and taste.

High-yield study points

  • The cornea provides much of the eye's refractive power; the lens changes shape for accommodation.
  • The retina converts light into neural signals; the optic nerve carries output toward the brain.
  • The auditory ossicles are malleus, incus and stapes.
  • The cochlea supports hearing; semicircular ducts and otolith organs contribute to balance.
  • Smell and taste are chemical senses that interact strongly in flavour perception.

Clinical connection

Sudden visual loss, severe eye pain, sudden hearing loss or acute neurological symptoms need urgent medical assessment. Anatomical knowledge supports understanding but does not replace examination.

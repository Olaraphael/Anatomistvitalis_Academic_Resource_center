Gross anatomy of the respiratory tract and lungs

The respiratory tract is divided into conducting passages and the gas-exchanging region. The nose, nasal cavity, pharynx, larynx, trachea, bronchi and conducting bronchioles condition and deliver air; respiratory bronchioles and alveoli participate in gas exchange. The right lung usually has three lobes and two fissures, while the left lung has two lobes, a cardiac notch and lingula. The root of each lung carries the main bronchus, pulmonary vessels, bronchial vessels, lymphatics and nerves. The pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood to the lungs, and pulmonary veins return oxygenated blood to the left atrium.

Learning objectives

  • Trace the conducting and respiratory portions of the airway from the trachea to the alveoli.
  • Compare the lobes, fissures and main bronchial anatomy of the right and left lungs.
  • Distinguish the lung hilum from the root and describe the basic pleural arrangement.
  • Relate bronchopulmonary segments and airway orientation to clinical examples such as aspiration and segmental disease.

High-yield study points

  • The trachea bifurcates at the carina, whose position changes with respiration and posture.
  • The right main bronchus is generally wider, shorter and more vertical than the left.
  • Bronchopulmonary segments are supplied by segmental bronchi and corresponding pulmonary arterial branches and are surgically meaningful units.
  • The pulmonary artery and pulmonary veins have different oxygenation roles from systemic arteries and veins.
  • The visceral pleura covers the lung; the parietal pleura lines the thoracic cavity.

Clinical connection

Aspiration patterns are influenced by posture and airway anatomy. Lobar collapse, pleural disease and airway obstruction are evaluated using examination and appropriate imaging.

Check your understanding

  1. How many lobes does each lung have?
    Reveal answer

    The right lung usually has three lobes; the left lung has two.

  2. Why is aspirated material more likely to enter the right main bronchus in many circumstances?
    Reveal answer

    The right main bronchus is generally wider, shorter and more vertical than the left, though posture and individual anatomy also matter.

  3. What is the difference between visceral and parietal pleura?
    Reveal answer

    Visceral pleura covers the lung surface; parietal pleura lines the thoracic wall, diaphragm and mediastinal surfaces.

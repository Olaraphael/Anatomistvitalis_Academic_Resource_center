PHYSIOLOGY · COURSE MAP

Physiology: how the human body works

Physiology is a complete biomedical course, not a single topic placed between anatomy lessons. It explains how cells, tissues, organs and organ systems function, how they regulate the internal environment, and how their mechanisms change during exercise, disease and treatment. Learn each system in a consistent sequence: normal function → regulatory mechanism → integration with other systems → clinical application → self-assessment.

Recommended foundation: cell physiology and homeostasis. Then study blood and body fluids, nerve and muscle, cardiovascular, respiratory, renal, gastrointestinal, endocrine and reproductive physiology. Return to integrated physiology to connect the systems.

How to study a physiology topic

  1. Define the function and the variable being regulated.
  2. Identify the structures and cells responsible.
  3. Trace the mechanism in the correct order, including channels, transporters, hormones or nerves where relevant.
  4. Explain feedback and how the response changes when the system is challenged.
  5. Apply the mechanism to a clinical example without confusing association with causation.
  6. Answer questions from memory, then check the explanation.

Self-check

Why is renal physiology closely linked to cardiovascular physiology?

The kidneys regulate extracellular-fluid volume and sodium balance, influencing venous return and blood pressure. Renal perfusion is also affected by arterial pressure and neurohormonal control, so the systems influence each other in both directions.

What is the difference between a mechanism and a function?

A function describes what a system accomplishes; a mechanism explains the physical, chemical or cellular steps by which it occurs.

Why should normal physiology be studied before pathophysiology?

A sound model of normal function makes it easier to identify what has changed, why symptoms occur and how a treatment may act.

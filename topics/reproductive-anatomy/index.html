Reproductive anatomy: male and female systems

The reproductive systems produce gametes and sex hormones and support fertilisation; the female reproductive tract also supports implantation, pregnancy and birth. In the male system, testes produce sperm and testosterone, the epididymides support sperm maturation, and ducts transport sperm toward the urethra. Accessory glands contribute seminal fluid. In the female system, ovaries produce oocytes and hormones; uterine tubes are usual sites of fertilisation; the uterus supports gestation; and the vagina forms a canal between the cervix and exterior. External genital anatomy, pelvic support and vascular supply are important for function. Individual anatomy varies, and anatomical descriptions should be respectful and precise.

Learning objectives

  • Identify the main organs and ducts of each reproductive system.
  • Trace sperm and oocyte pathways at a basic level.
  • Relate reproductive organ structure to function and clinical terminology.

High-yield study points

  • The epididymis is important for sperm maturation and storage.
  • The uterine tube has infundibulum, ampulla, isthmus and uterine part; fertilisation most often occurs in the ampulla.
  • The uterine wall comprises endometrium, myometrium and perimetrium where present.
  • The prostate surrounds the proximal prostatic urethra; enlargement can affect urinary flow.
  • Pelvic organs have variable peritoneal relations and support structures.

Clinical connection

Reproductive symptoms can have many causes. Fertility, sexually transmitted infections, masses and pain should be assessed with consent, privacy and appropriate professional care.

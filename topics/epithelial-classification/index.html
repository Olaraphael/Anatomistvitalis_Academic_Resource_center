Epithelial tissue: classification and recognition

Epithelia are classified by the number of cell layers and the shape of the cells at the free surface. Simple epithelium has one layer; stratified epithelium has multiple layers. Pseudostratified epithelium appears multilayered because nuclei lie at different heights, but all cells contact the basement membrane. Transitional epithelium changes shape as the urinary tract stretches. Squamous cells are flat, cuboidal cells are approximately as tall as they are wide, and columnar cells are taller than they are wide. Simple squamous epithelium supports rapid diffusion and filtration in sites such as alveoli and endothelium. Simple cuboidal epithelium commonly supports secretion and absorption in small ducts and tubules. Simple columnar epithelium lines much of the gastrointestinal tract. Stratified squamous epithelium protects against abrasion; keratinised epidermis reduces water loss, while non-keratinised forms line moist passages. Look for the basement membrane, cell layers, apical surface, nuclei and any cilia, goblet cells or keratin. Glands are epithelial derivatives; exocrine glands release products through ducts or onto surfaces, while endocrine glands release hormones into interstitial fluid and blood.

Learning objectives

  • Classify epithelia by layers and surface cell shape.
  • Relate common epithelial types to their location and function.
  • Distinguish exocrine from endocrine gland organisation.

High-yield study points

  • Simple = one layer; stratified = multiple layers; pseudostratified = all cells contact the basement membrane.
  • Squamous cells are flat, cuboidal cells are roughly cube-shaped, and columnar cells are taller.
  • Transitional epithelium accommodates stretching in urinary passages.
  • Cilia move material; microvilli increase surface area; goblet cells secrete mucus.
  • Exocrine glands use ducts or surface release; endocrine glands release hormones into the internal environment.

Clinical connection

Epithelial metaplasia, dysplasia and carcinoma are understood against normal epithelial organisation. Histological diagnosis must be made by trained professionals using appropriate specimens and context.

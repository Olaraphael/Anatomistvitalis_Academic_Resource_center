Cardiac conduction system and electrocardiogram basics

The cardiac conduction system coordinates atrial and ventricular activation. The sinoatrial node commonly initiates the heartbeat; excitation spreads through atrial myocardium to the atrioventricular node, where conduction slows, then travels through the atrioventricular bundle, right and left bundle branches and Purkinje fibres. This sequence supports atrial contraction before ventricular contraction and rapid, coordinated ventricular activation. An electrocardiogram records voltage differences at the body surface rather than directly recording mechanical contraction. The P wave usually reflects atrial depolarisation, the QRS complex ventricular depolarisation, and the T wave ventricular repolarisation.

Learning objectives

  • Trace the normal cardiac conduction pathway.
  • Relate ECG waves to electrical events.
  • Distinguish electrical activation from mechanical contraction.

High-yield study points

  • The SA node is the usual dominant pacemaker because it normally fires faster than subsidiary pacemakers.
  • The AV node delay allows time for ventricular filling after atrial activation.
  • The QRS complex masks most atrial repolarisation on a standard ECG.
  • Electrical depolarisation precedes the associated mechanical contraction.
  • ECG interpretation requires rhythm, rate, intervals, axis and waveform assessment in clinical context.

Clinical connection

A rhythm strip or single ECG feature rarely gives the whole diagnosis. Chest pain, fainting or severe breathlessness with possible cardiac causes requires urgent assessment.

Check your understanding

  1. Where does normal sinus rhythm usually begin?
    Answer and explanation

    In the sinoatrial node, whose spontaneous depolarisation normally sets the heart rate.

  2. Why is conduction delayed at the atrioventricular node?
    Answer and explanation

    The delay allows time for atrial contraction and ventricular filling before ventricular activation. Conduction properties are influenced by autonomic tone and other factors.

  3. What is the functional advantage of the His–Purkinje system?
    Answer and explanation

    It distributes excitation rapidly through the ventricles, supporting coordinated ventricular depolarisation and contraction.

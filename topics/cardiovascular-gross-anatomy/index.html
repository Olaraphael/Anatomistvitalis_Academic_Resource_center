Gross anatomy of the heart and great vessels

The heart lies in the middle mediastinum, enclosed by the pericardium. It has right and left atria and ventricles separated by septa. The tricuspid valve guards the right atrioventricular opening, the pulmonary valve the right ventricular outflow, the mitral valve the left atrioventricular opening and the aortic valve the left ventricular outflow. The coronary arteries arise from the ascending aorta and supply the myocardium; venous blood drains mainly through cardiac veins into the coronary sinus and right atrium. The superior and inferior venae cavae return systemic blood to the right atrium, while pulmonary veins return oxygenated blood to the left atrium.

Learning objectives

  • Trace blood through the chambers and valves.
  • Identify the great vessels and coronary circulation.
  • Relate cardiac surface anatomy to clinical examination.

High-yield study points

  • The right heart pumps blood through the pulmonary circulation; the left heart pumps through the systemic circulation.
  • The apex is formed mainly by the left ventricle and is normally directed inferolaterally to the left.
  • The coronary arterial pattern varies; dominance is commonly described by the origin of the posterior interventricular artery.
  • The fibrous skeleton supports valve openings and electrically insulates atrial from ventricular myocardium except through the normal conduction pathway.
  • Heart sounds arise largely from valve closure and associated vibrations, not from valves actively opening.

Clinical connection

Coronary occlusion can cause myocardial ischaemia or infarction. Surface listening areas reflect sound transmission and do not exactly match the anatomical position of each valve.

Check your understanding

  1. Which chamber receives systemic venous blood through the venae cavae?
    Reveal answer

    The right atrium.

  2. Which valve lies between the left atrium and left ventricle?
    Reveal answer

    The mitral (bicuspid) valve.

  3. Why does the left ventricular wall normally have a thicker myocardium than the right?
    Reveal answer

    It must generate higher pressure to drive blood through the systemic circulation, whereas the right ventricle pumps through the lower-pressure pulmonary circulation.

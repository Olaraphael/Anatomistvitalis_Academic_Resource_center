Bone remodelling, calcium balance and fracture healing

Bone is living tissue continually remodelled by osteoclast-mediated resorption and osteoblast-mediated formation. Osteocytes embedded in matrix sense mechanical strain and coordinate local responses. Calcium and phosphate availability, mechanical loading, age, nutrition and hormones including parathyroid hormone, vitamin D and calcitonin influence skeletal metabolism. Fracture repair generally progresses through inflammatory haematoma, soft callus, hard callus and remodelling, though the timing and details vary with fracture pattern, blood supply, stability and individual health. Bone adapts to loading but can lose strength with disuse or disease.

Learning objectives

  • Explain the roles of osteoclasts, osteoblasts and osteocytes.
  • Summarise hormonal control of calcium and phosphate.
  • Outline the broad phases of fracture healing.

High-yield study points

  • Osteoclasts resorb mineralised matrix; osteoblasts synthesise osteoid and support mineralisation.
  • Vitamin D supports intestinal calcium and phosphate absorption; parathyroid hormone raises blood calcium through actions on kidney and bone and promotes renal vitamin D activation.
  • Mechanical loading influences remodelling, while prolonged unloading can contribute to bone loss.
  • Fracture healing depends on perfusion, stability, alignment, infection status and systemic factors.
  • Remodelling can continue after clinical union as bone adapts to the forces placed on it.

Clinical connection

Poor blood supply, infection, smoking, malnutrition and some medications can impair healing. Suspected fractures need appropriate immobilisation and clinical assessment.

Check your understanding: remodelling and fracture healing

  1. Which cell is primarily responsible for resorbing mineralised bone?

    Reveal answer and explanation

    Osteoclast. Osteoclasts are multinucleated cells that acidify and enzymatically digest mineralised matrix. Osteoblasts build bone matrix, while osteocytes are embedded mechanosensory cells that help coordinate remodelling.

  2. Why does parathyroid hormone not simply mean “bone breakdown” in every context?

    Reveal answer and explanation

    PTH increases serum calcium through renal effects, stimulation of active vitamin D production and effects on bone remodelling. Its net skeletal effect depends on the pattern and duration of exposure; intermittent therapeutic exposure differs from sustained elevation.

  3. Name the broad phases of secondary fracture healing.

    Reveal answer and explanation

    Haematoma/inflammation, soft callus formation, hard callus formation and remodelling. The phases overlap; stability, vascular supply, fracture pattern, infection and systemic health affect progress.

  4. Why can prolonged immobilisation or unloading reduce bone mass?

    Reveal answer and explanation

    Bone adapts to mechanical loading. Reduced loading alters remodelling signals and can shift the balance toward resorption, contributing to disuse bone loss.

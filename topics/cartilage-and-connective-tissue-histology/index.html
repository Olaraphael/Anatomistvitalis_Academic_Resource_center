Histology of cartilage, tendons, ligaments and bone

Cartilage is an avascular connective tissue with chondrocytes in lacunae embedded in an extracellular matrix rich in proteoglycans and collagen. Hyaline cartilage occurs at articular surfaces, in costal cartilages and in many respiratory structures; elastic cartilage contains abundant elastic fibres; fibrocartilage resists compression and tension in structures such as intervertebral discs and menisci. Tendons and many ligaments contain dense regular connective tissue with aligned collagen bundles. Bone matrix is mineralised and contains osteocytes in lacunae linked by canaliculi; its lamellar organisation differs from unmineralised connective tissue.

Learning objectives

  • Compare hyaline, elastic and fibrocartilage.
  • Recognise dense regular connective tissue in tendons and many ligaments.
  • Describe key microscopic features of compact bone.

High-yield study points

  • Cartilage lacks blood vessels; nutrition occurs by diffusion, which contributes to limited repair capacity.
  • Articular cartilage normally lacks a perichondrium.
  • Tendon collagen bundles align with tensile load and are interspersed with elongated fibroblast-lineage cells.
  • Osteons in compact bone contain concentric lamellae around central canals.
  • Decalcification allows bone sectioning but removes mineral and therefore changes what can be demonstrated histologically.

Clinical connection

Cartilage injury often heals poorly compared with well-vascularised tissues. Histological preparation can alter appearance, so observations should be interpreted with method and tissue context.

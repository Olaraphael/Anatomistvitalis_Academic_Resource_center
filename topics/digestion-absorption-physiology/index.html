Digestive enzymes, nutrient absorption and portal circulation

Digestion breaks food polymers into absorbable molecules through mechanical processing, acid, bile and enzymes. Carbohydrates are digested to monosaccharides, proteins to amino acids and small peptides, and lipids to fatty acids and monoacylglycerols that form mixed micelles with bile salts. Enterocytes absorb nutrients through specific transporters and release many water-soluble nutrients into portal blood. Most long-chain dietary lipids are reassembled into triglycerides and packaged into chylomicrons that enter intestinal lymphatic lacteals before reaching the bloodstream. Absorption varies by nutrient and intestinal segment.

Learning objectives

  • Summarise digestion of carbohydrates, proteins and fats.
  • Explain the different transport routes for water-soluble nutrients and chylomicrons.
  • Describe the role of bile and the hepatic portal circulation.

High-yield study points

  • Salivary and pancreatic amylases digest starch; brush-border enzymes complete carbohydrate digestion.
  • Pepsin begins protein digestion in the stomach; pancreatic proteases and brush-border peptidases continue it in the small intestine.
  • Bile salts emulsify lipids and support micelle formation but are not digestive enzymes.
  • Most monosaccharides and amino acids enter portal blood, while chylomicrons enter lymph first.
  • The small intestine's folds, villi and microvilli create a large absorptive surface.

Clinical connection

Malabsorption can result from mucosal disease, pancreatic insufficiency, bile problems or altered intestinal anatomy. Symptoms and tests must be interpreted together.

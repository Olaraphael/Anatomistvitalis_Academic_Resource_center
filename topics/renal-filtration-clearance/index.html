Glomerular filtration, renal clearance and urine formation

Glomerular filtration moves water and small solutes from glomerular capillary blood into Bowman's space across a selective filtration barrier. Filtration depends on hydraulic and oncotic pressures, filtration surface area and barrier properties. The filtered fluid is modified along the nephron by tubular reabsorption and secretion. Renal clearance expresses the virtual volume of plasma cleared of a substance per unit time and can help characterise renal handling when assumptions are met. The kidneys regulate waste excretion, electrolytes, acid–base balance, blood volume and several endocrine functions.

Learning objectives

  • Describe the glomerular filtration barrier and forces affecting filtration.
  • Distinguish filtration, reabsorption, secretion and excretion.
  • Define renal clearance conceptually.

High-yield study points

  • The filtration barrier includes fenestrated endothelium, glomerular basement membrane and podocyte slit diaphragms.
  • Net filtration depends on the balance of hydrostatic and oncotic pressures across the filtration barrier.
  • Filtered load depends on plasma concentration and glomerular filtration rate for freely filtered substances.
  • Reabsorption returns substances to blood; secretion transfers substances from blood into tubular fluid.
  • Estimated GFR calculations are useful but have limitations and should be interpreted with age, body composition and clinical context.

Clinical connection

A change in serum creatinine may reflect filtration, production, distribution or medication effects. Kidney function assessment uses trends, urine findings and the overall clinical picture.

Check your understanding

  1. What does renal clearance estimate?
    Reveal answer

    The volume of plasma from which a substance is completely removed per unit time, as inferred from its urinary excretion and plasma concentration.

  2. How does inulin clearance relate to GFR under ideal measurement conditions?
    Reveal answer

    Inulin is freely filtered and neither reabsorbed nor secreted, so its clearance estimates glomerular filtration rate.

  3. What is the general excretion equation?
    Reveal answer

    Excretion = filtration − reabsorption + secretion.

Renal histology: nephron, glomerulus and tubular segments

Each nephron begins with a renal corpuscle, consisting of a glomerular capillary tuft enclosed by glomerular capsule, followed by proximal tubule, loop of Henle and distal tubule. Collecting ducts receive fluid from multiple nephrons and help finalise urine concentration. The filtration barrier includes fenestrated glomerular endothelium, a specialised glomerular basement membrane and podocyte slit diaphragms. Proximal tubular cells have a prominent brush border to support reabsorption; distal segments have different transporter profiles and less prominent apical microvilli. The juxtaglomerular apparatus helps regulate renin release and glomerular filtration.

Learning objectives

  • Identify the components of the renal corpuscle.
  • Compare proximal and distal tubular histology.
  • Explain the basic role of the juxtaglomerular apparatus.

High-yield study points

  • Bowman's space collects filtrate from the glomerular filtration barrier.
  • Podocyte foot processes form filtration slits bridged by slit diaphragms.
  • The proximal tubule reabsorbs most filtered water and solutes under normal conditions.
  • The macula densa senses tubular sodium chloride delivery and communicates with neighbouring vascular and renin-producing cells.
  • Cortical and juxtamedullary nephrons differ in loop length and contribute differently to concentration mechanisms.

Clinical connection

Proteinuria, haematuria and reduced filtration can reflect different renal processes. Urine and blood tests must be interpreted alongside symptoms and clinical history.

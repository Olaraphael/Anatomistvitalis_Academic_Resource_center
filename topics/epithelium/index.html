Epithelial tissue

Epithelium covers body surfaces, lines cavities and forms glands. Its cells are closely packed, rest on a basement membrane, show apical-basal polarity and are avascular, receiving nutrients by diffusion from underlying tissue.

Learning objectives

  • Classify epithelia by number of layers and the shape of surface cells.
  • Relate epithelial structure to protection, absorption, secretion, diffusion or transport.
  • Distinguish cilia, microvilli and stereocilia by structure and function.
  • Recognise common epithelial types in representative organs and explain the role of the basement membrane.

High-yield study points

  • Simple epithelium has one cell layer; stratified epithelium has multiple layers.
  • Squamous, cuboidal and columnar describe cell shape.
  • Cilia move material along a surface; microvilli increase surface area.
  • Glandular epithelium specialises in secretion.
  • Epithelia are classified by both layer number and cell shape.

Clinical connection

The epithelial type reflects function: thin simple squamous epithelium supports diffusion, while stratified squamous epithelium protects against abrasion.

Check your understanding

  1. What does “stratified” mean when describing epithelium?
    Reveal answer

    It has more than one cell layer; classification is based on the shape of the most superficial cells.

  2. What is the main function of microvilli?
    Reveal answer

    They increase apical surface area, supporting absorption or other surface exchange.

  3. Why is the basement membrane important?
    Reveal answer

    It supports and anchors epithelium, helps organise the interface with connective tissue and contributes to selective filtration in specialised sites.

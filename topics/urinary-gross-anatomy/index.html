Gross anatomy of the kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra

The urinary system comprises paired kidneys, ureters, the urinary bladder and urethra. Kidneys are retroperitoneal organs with a cortex, medulla, renal pyramids, calyces and renal pelvis. At the hilum, the renal vein, renal artery and renal pelvis/ureter have a characteristic relationship that varies somewhat. Ureters carry urine to the bladder through peristalsis and enter the bladder obliquely. The bladder stores urine; the urethra conducts it out of the body. Urethral length and relationships differ between sexes, affecting some infection and catheterisation risks.

Learning objectives

  • Identify major gross kidney structures and the urinary pathway.
  • Describe the course of the ureters and organisation of the bladder.
  • Relate urinary anatomy to common obstruction sites.

High-yield study points

  • The kidneys lie retroperitoneally; the right kidney is often slightly lower because of the liver.
  • Urine flows from collecting ducts to papillae, minor and major calyces, renal pelvis, ureter, bladder and urethra.
  • Ureteric narrowing sites commonly include the pelviureteric junction, crossing of the pelvic brim and entry through the bladder wall.
  • The detrusor is smooth muscle in the bladder wall; continence depends on coordinated bladder, sphincter and neural function.
  • The nephron is the functional microscopic unit responsible for filtration and tubular processing.

Clinical connection

Urinary stones can obstruct the ureter and cause colicky pain. Urinary retention, haematuria and suspected infection require clinical evaluation rather than self-diagnosis.

Check your understanding: urinary gross anatomy

  1. What is the usual sequence of urine flow from the renal pelvis to the exterior?
    Answer and explanation

    Renal pelvis → ureter → urinary bladder → urethra. Urine passes through the collecting system before reaching the renal pelvis.

  2. Why do ureters have clinically important narrowing points?
    Answer and explanation

    Common constrictions occur near the pelvi-ureteric junction, where the ureter crosses the pelvic brim/iliac vessels, and at the ureterovesical junction. These are common sites where calculi may lodge, though stones can pass at other sites too.

  3. How does the kidney's position relate to the peritoneum?
    Answer and explanation

    The kidneys are retroperitoneal organs on the posterior abdominal wall. Their posterior relations include the diaphragm and posterior abdominal wall muscles, with the ribs contributing protection.

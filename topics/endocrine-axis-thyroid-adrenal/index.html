Endocrine feedback: thyroid and adrenal axes

Endocrine axes use coordinated signalling between hypothalamus, pituitary and peripheral glands. In the hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid axis, thyrotropin-releasing hormone stimulates pituitary TSH, which promotes thyroid hormone synthesis and release; circulating thyroid hormones feed back negatively on the hypothalamus and pituitary. The hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis involves CRH, ACTH and cortisol, with cortisol providing negative feedback and showing a daily rhythm influenced by sleep and stress. These systems adjust metabolism, stress responses and many organ functions, but the pattern of regulation differs among hormones.

Learning objectives

  • Trace the hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid axis.
  • Trace the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis.
  • Explain negative feedback and why hormone tests need context.

High-yield study points

  • TSH stimulates thyroid hormone synthesis and release; T3 and T4 exert negative feedback on upstream signalling.
  • Cortisol secretion follows circadian and ultradian patterns and changes with illness, stress and medication.
  • Hormone concentration depends on secretion, binding proteins, metabolism and clearance.
  • Primary gland failure and central axis disorders can produce different combinations of target-gland and pituitary hormone results.
  • Interpretation depends on the assay, reference interval, symptoms, medication and timing.

Clinical connection

Thyroid and adrenal laboratory results should not be interpreted from one value alone when context is missing. Hormone replacement and suppression therapies require professional monitoring.

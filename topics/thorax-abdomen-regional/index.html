Regional anatomy of the thorax and abdomen: an orientation

Regional anatomy examines structures together rather than in isolation. In the thorax, the thoracic wall encloses pleural cavities and the mediastinum; the diaphragm separates the thorax from the abdomen. The abdominal wall protects and supports viscera, while peritoneal relationships, mesenteries, fascia, vessels and nerves help explain organ mobility and routes of spread.

Learning objectives

  • Orient the thoracic and abdominal cavities and identify major surface landmarks.
  • Describe how the diaphragm separates thoracic and abdominal cavities while permitting passage of major structures.
  • Distinguish thoracic organs from abdominal viscera and relate their positions to body regions.
  • Use surface anatomy as a starting point for systematic regional examination.

High-yield study points

  • The thoracic wall includes ribs, costal cartilages, sternum, thoracic vertebrae, intercostal muscles, fascia, vessels and nerves.
  • The mediastinum is divided into superior and inferior parts; the inferior mediastinum is commonly described as anterior, middle and posterior compartments.
  • The lungs occupy pleural cavities; the heart lies within the pericardium in the middle mediastinum.
  • The abdominal wall includes skin, superficial fascia, muscle-aponeurotic layers, transversalis fascia, extraperitoneal tissue and parietal peritoneum.
  • The peritoneum forms visceral and parietal layers; mesenteries suspend many organs and provide routes for vessels, nerves and lymphatics.
  • Intraperitoneal, retroperitoneal and secondarily retroperitoneal describe relationships to the peritoneum, not whether an organ is literally inside a free cavity.
  • Surface landmarks guide examination but do not precisely delimit every organ because organs move with respiration, filling and posture.

Clinical connection

Understanding layers and compartments helps explain hernias, pleural procedures, abdominal fluid distribution and why infection or bleeding may spread along specific anatomical planes.

Check your understanding

  1. Which muscle forms the principal partition between thoracic and abdominal cavities?
    Reveal answer

    The diaphragm.

  2. Which major organ lies mainly in the right upper abdomen beneath the diaphragm?
    Reveal answer

    The liver, although its anatomy extends across the midline.

  3. Why should surface regions not be treated as exact maps of internal organs?
    Reveal answer

    Organs vary in size, position and movement, and surface divisions are orientation aids rather than precise boundaries of every viscus.

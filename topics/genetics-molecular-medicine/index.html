Human genetics and molecular biology foundations

Human genetic information is encoded in DNA, organised into chromosomes and accessed through gene expression. Transcription produces RNA from a DNA template; translation uses messenger RNA to direct protein synthesis at ribosomes. Variants may alter protein sequence, gene regulation or RNA processing, and their effects depend on the gene, variant and biological context. Inheritance patterns include autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, X-linked and mitochondrial patterns, but real traits can also be polygenic and influenced by environment. Gene expression is regulated at multiple levels, so genotype does not always predict phenotype with certainty.

Learning objectives

  • Describe the central flow of genetic information.
  • Distinguish genes, alleles, chromosomes and variants.
  • Recognise common inheritance patterns and their limitations.

High-yield study points

  • DNA is transcribed into RNA; messenger RNA is translated into a polypeptide sequence.
  • A gene variant may be benign, pathogenic or of uncertain significance depending on evidence.
  • Autosomal recessive conditions typically require pathogenic variants affecting both copies of a gene.
  • Dominant inheritance does not mean every carrier has identical severity or age of onset.
  • Environment, epigenetic regulation and interactions among genes can modify phenotype.

Clinical connection

Genetic test results require careful interpretation, consent and attention to family implications. A variant of uncertain significance should not be treated as proof of disease without supporting evidence.

Check your understanding

  1. What is the difference between genotype and phenotype?
    Reveal answer

    Genotype refers to genetic constitution or variants; phenotype refers to observable traits arising from genetic, environmental and developmental influences.

  2. What is a pathogenic variant?
    Reveal answer

    A genetic variant supported by evidence as contributing to disease; interpretation depends on classification standards, inheritance, phenotype and evidence.

  3. Why does the presence of a genetic variant not always predict disease severity precisely?
    Reveal answer

    Penetrance, variable expressivity, modifier genes, environment and chance influence how variants manifest.

Foot and ankle anatomy: arches, gait and tendon pathways

The ankle mortise is formed by the distal tibia and fibula around the talus, enabling primarily dorsiflexion and plantarflexion. The subtalar and other intertarsal joints contribute to inversion, eversion and adaptation to uneven surfaces. The foot has medial and lateral longitudinal arches and a transverse arch supported by bone geometry, plantar fascia, ligaments and muscles. The Achilles tendon transmits force from gastrocnemius and soleus to the calcaneus. Tendons passing behind the medial malleolus include the tibialis posterior, flexor digitorum longus, posterior tibial vessels, tibial nerve and flexor hallucis longus, remembered in that order from anterior to posterior using a standard mnemonic.

Learning objectives

  • Identify major ankle and foot joints.
  • Describe the arches and their support mechanisms.
  • Relate major tendon pathways to movement and stability.

High-yield study points

  • Dorsiflexion raises the dorsum of the foot; plantarflexion points the foot downward.
  • Inversion turns the sole medially; eversion turns it laterally.
  • The plantar aponeurosis helps support the longitudinal arch and contributes to elastic energy storage during gait.
  • The lateral ankle ligament complex is commonly injured during inversion sprains.
  • The tibial nerve passes through the tarsal tunnel; compression can cause plantar foot symptoms.

Clinical connection

Ankle sprains, Achilles tendon rupture and stress fractures have different examination patterns. Inability to bear weight or marked deformity after injury warrants prompt evaluation.

Check your understanding

  1. Which ligament complex is commonly injured in an inversion ankle sprain?
    Reveal answer

    The lateral ligament complex, particularly the anterior talofibular ligament in many common sprains.

  2. Which bone transmits body weight from the tibia to the foot at the ankle joint?
    Reveal answer

    The talus.

  3. What is the functional role of the arches of the foot?
    Reveal answer

    They distribute load, provide a resilient base, and help store and release energy during locomotion.

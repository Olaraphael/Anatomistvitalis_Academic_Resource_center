Connective tissue, matrix, cartilage and bone

Connective tissues are characterised by cells distributed within an extracellular matrix. The matrix contains ground substance and fibres, especially collagen, elastic fibres and reticular fibres in different combinations. Fibroblasts produce many matrix components; immune cells, adipocytes and specialised cells may also be present. Unlike epithelia, many connective tissues have abundant matrix and variable vascularity. Loose connective tissue supports and cushions structures and permits diffusion; dense regular connective tissue, such as many tendons, has aligned collagen bundles adapted to tensile load in one direction. Dense irregular connective tissue resists forces from multiple directions. Adipose tissue stores energy, cushions and signals. Cartilage has chondrocytes in lacunae and a firm hydrated matrix; it is generally avascular and receives nutrients by diffusion. Bone matrix is mineralised, with osteoblasts forming bone, osteoclasts resorbing it and osteocytes embedded within it. Blood is a specialised fluid connective tissue. In a slide, distinguish cell type and matrix pattern. Collagen bundles, lacunae, mineralised lamellae and marrow spaces provide clues, but preparation can alter appearance.

Learning objectives

  • Describe the main components of extracellular matrix.
  • Compare loose, dense, adipose, cartilage, bone and blood connective tissues.
  • Explain the roles of major bone cell types.

High-yield study points

  • Fibres and ground substance give connective tissues their mechanical and diffusion properties.
  • Dense regular connective tissue aligns collagen with the main direction of force.
  • Cartilage is generally avascular; nutrients diffuse through its matrix.
  • Osteoblasts form bone, osteoclasts resorb bone, and osteocytes help maintain and sense bone tissue.
  • Bone is continuously remodelled in response to loading and systemic signals.

Clinical connection

Tendons, cartilage and bone heal differently partly because of matrix structure and blood supply. Injury, age, loading and disease all influence repair.

Check your understanding

  1. What are the main components of connective tissue?
    Reveal answer

    Cells and extracellular matrix, which includes ground substance and fibres such as collagen, elastic and reticular fibres.

  2. Which connective-tissue cell is especially associated with producing extracellular matrix?
    Reveal answer

    Fibroblasts in many connective tissues.

  3. Why does cartilage generally repair slowly?
    Reveal answer

    It is avascular and has limited access to repair cells and nutrients, so healing capacity is restricted.

Anatomical planes, axes, sections and orientation

A plane is an imaginary flat surface through the body; a section is the actual or reconstructed cut surface seen after a structure is divided or imaged. Planes help describe position, movement and the orientation of CT, MRI, ultrasound and anatomical specimens. A plane and an axis are related but not interchangeable: an axis is a line around which a movement occurs, while a plane describes the direction in which movement or section is considered.

High-yield study points

  • The median (midsagittal) plane divides the body into equal right and left halves; parasagittal planes divide it into unequal right and left portions.
  • A frontal or coronal plane divides anterior from posterior portions.
  • A transverse, horizontal or axial plane divides superior from inferior portions when the body is in anatomical position.
  • An oblique plane passes through a structure at an angle not aligned with the standard planes.
  • Longitudinal sections follow the long axis of a structure; cross-sections are approximately perpendicular to that axis, and oblique sections cut at an angle.
  • Flexion and extension are commonly described in sagittal planes around a mediolateral axis; abduction and adduction commonly occur in frontal planes around an anteroposterior axis.
  • Rotation is often described in the transverse plane around a longitudinal axis, but actual joint movement can combine planes and axes.
  • Image orientation must be checked using the modality's display convention and patient markers; do not assume every displayed image has the same left-right orientation.

Clinical connection

In cross-sectional imaging, recognising the plane helps identify whether a slice shows the body from above, from the front, or from the side. Radiology reports should follow the displayed patient orientation rather than the viewer's intuition.

Metabolism, ATP and energy balance

Metabolism is the sum of chemical reactions that maintain life. Catabolic pathways break molecules down and capture some released energy in ATP and reduced coenzymes; anabolic pathways use energy and precursors to build cellular components. Glycolysis converts glucose to pyruvate in the cytosol; under aerobic conditions, pyruvate-derived acetyl-CoA enters the citric acid cycle and reducing equivalents support oxidative phosphorylation in mitochondria. Fatty acids can be oxidised through beta-oxidation, while amino acids may enter energy pathways after nitrogen handling. Metabolic flux changes with feeding, fasting, exercise, hormones and tissue demands.

Learning objectives

  • Define catabolism, anabolism and ATP's role.
  • Outline the main stages of glucose oxidation.
  • Explain why metabolism differs among tissues and physiological states.

High-yield study points

  • ATP couples energy-releasing reactions to energy-requiring cellular work.
  • Glycolysis occurs in the cytosol and does not directly require oxygen.
  • The citric acid cycle generates reduced coenzymes that supply electrons to the respiratory chain.
  • Oxidative phosphorylation depends on an intact proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
  • Insulin, glucagon, catecholamines and other signals coordinate fuel storage and mobilisation.

Clinical connection

Metabolic disorders may arise from enzyme defects, hormonal dysregulation, nutritional factors or organ dysfunction. Biochemical pathways help explain laboratory patterns but do not independently establish a diagnosis.

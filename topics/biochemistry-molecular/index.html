Biochemistry and molecular biology for anatomy and physiology

Biochemistry explains how molecules support living structure and function; molecular biology examines how genetic information is stored, expressed and regulated. Water, ions, proteins, lipids, carbohydrates and nucleic acids underpin cellular organisation, signalling, energy conversion and tissue maintenance. These foundations help connect microscopic anatomy to physiology and disease.

Learning objectives

  • Explain how molecular structure influences biological function.
  • Distinguish carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and nucleic acids by basic structure and role.
  • Describe the roles of enzymes, ATP and cellular compartmentalisation in metabolism.
  • Connect molecular mechanisms to cellular physiology and disease without treating one pathway as an isolated system.

High-yield study points

  • Water is a polar solvent; hydrogen bonding and solute concentration influence molecular interactions, osmotic movement and protein structure.
  • Proteins perform structural, transport, enzymatic, contractile, immune and signalling roles; function depends on amino-acid sequence and three-dimensional conformation.
  • Lipids form membranes, store energy and contribute to signalling; phospholipids form bilayers because they have both hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions.
  • Carbohydrates supply energy, store fuel and contribute to cell recognition and extracellular matrix structures.
  • DNA stores genetic information; transcription produces RNA from DNA templates, and translation uses messenger RNA to direct protein synthesis.
  • Enzymes lower activation energy and alter reaction rates without being consumed; their activity depends on factors such as temperature, pH, substrate and regulation.
  • ATP transfers usable chemical energy to cellular processes, while metabolic pathways integrate energy production and biosynthesis.

Clinical connection

Molecular mechanisms help explain inherited conditions, enzyme deficiencies, membrane disorders and the action of medicines, but a molecular finding must be interpreted in its biological and clinical context.

Check your understanding

  1. What is the main catalytic role of an enzyme?
    Reveal answer

    It increases reaction rate by lowering activation energy without being consumed overall in the reaction.

  2. Why is ATP described as an energy-coupling molecule?
    Reveal answer

    Its hydrolysis can be coupled to energetically unfavourable processes, enabling reactions such as active transport and biosynthesis.

  3. Which class of biomolecule stores genetic information?
    Reveal answer

    Nucleic acids, especially DNA and RNA.

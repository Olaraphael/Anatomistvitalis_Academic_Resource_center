Recommended learning order

Do not jump between unrelated articles. Work through the foundations first, then development and tissues, then regional and systemic gross anatomy, followed by neuroanatomy and clinical imaging. Revisit histology and embryology when they explain an organ's adult structure.

Visual study: real anatomy and microscopy

These are real specimen or microscope images from open-source repositories. Captions explain what can be identified; the associated lesson supplies the detailed anatomy.

How each anatomy article should be built

  • Precise definition and learning outcomes.
  • Developmental, gross, microscopic, neurovascular and functional relationships where relevant.
  • Real specimen photographs or micrographs where useful, plus labelled diagrams for structures that cannot be reliably identified from a photograph.
  • Clinical correlations and common examination errors.
  • Self-test questions with explained answers and credible further reading.

Images are not interchangeable: a photograph demonstrates real appearance, a labelled diagram teaches identification, a micrograph shows tissue architecture, and radiology teaches cross-sectional relationships. The article should select the appropriate visual type.

Study standard: use the article explanations alongside labelled visuals, self-test questions and cited further reading. Open educational images are credited on the page and in the image-credit register. Report broken links or suspected inaccuracies through the site owner contact information on the main page.

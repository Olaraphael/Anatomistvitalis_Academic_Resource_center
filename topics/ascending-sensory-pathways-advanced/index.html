Ascending sensory pathways: dorsal column, spinothalamic and trigeminal

26 min studyFree learning resourceIncludes memory aid

Lesson overview

The dorsal column–medial lemniscus pathway carries discriminative touch, vibration and conscious proprioception from the body. First-order fibres ascend ipsilaterally in fasciculus gracilis (lower body) or cuneatus (upper body), synapse in the medulla and cross as internal arcuate fibres before reaching the thalamus and somatosensory cortex. The anterolateral system, including the spinothalamic tract, carries pain and temperature; fibres commonly cross through the anterior white commissure within a few spinal segments. Facial sensation travels mainly through trigeminal pathways. The crossing point is essential for lesion localisation.

Learning objectives

  • Compare dorsal column and anterolateral modalities.
  • Trace where major sensory pathways cross.
  • Predict broad deficits from spinal or brain lesions.

High-yield points

  • DCML fibres ascend ipsilaterally in spinal cord and cross in the caudal medulla.
  • Spinothalamic fibres usually cross in the spinal cord near the entry level.
  • The thalamus relays most conscious somatic sensory information to primary somatosensory cortex.
  • Dermatomes, peripheral nerves, spinal segments and examination refine localisation.

Memory aid / mnemonic

“DCML crosses in the medulla; pain and temperature cross in the spinal cord.” DCML = discriminative touch, vibration, conscious proprioception.

Study tip: hide this section, explain the concept aloud, then check whether you remembered the sequence and its rationale.

Clinical connection

A hemicord lesion may cause ipsilateral loss of vibration/proprioception and contralateral pain-temperature loss below the lesion, with variation by level and extent.

Two major body sensory pathways

Dorsal column–medial lemniscus (DCML)

This pathway carries discriminative touch, vibration and conscious proprioception. First-order neuron cell bodies lie in dorsal root ganglia; their central processes ascend ipsilaterally in the fasciculi gracilis (lower body, medial) and cuneatus (upper body, lateral, present above the lower thoracic levels) to the gracile and cuneate nuclei in the medulla. Second-order fibres decussate as internal arcuate fibres and ascend as the medial lemniscus to the thalamus. Third-order neurons project to primary somatosensory cortex.

Anterolateral / spinothalamic system

This system carries pain, temperature and aspects of crude touch. First-order fibres enter the dorsal horn and may travel a short distance in Lissauer’s tract before synapsing. Second-order neurons cross through the anterior white commissure, usually within a few spinal segments, then ascend contralaterally in the anterolateral system to thalamic and other targets.

How to reason from a lesion

DCML fibres cross in the medulla, so a spinal cord lesion typically affects vibration and position sense on the same side below the lesion. Spinothalamic fibres cross near their spinal entry, so a cord lesion may impair pain and temperature on the opposite side beginning a short distance below the lesion. Brain lesions above the relevant decussation generally produce contralateral body sensory deficits. Actual patterns depend on lesion size, level and neighbouring structures.

Face sensation uses trigeminal pathways rather than simply continuing through the body’s dorsal columns. The trigeminal sensory nuclei and thalamic relays must be considered for facial findings.

Self-test

  1. Where do DCML second-order fibres cross?
  2. Where do spinothalamic fibres usually cross?
  3. Which pathway carries vibration and conscious proprioception?
Answers with reasoning

1. In the caudal medulla as internal arcuate fibres. 2. In the spinal cord through the anterior white commissure, near the entry level. 3. DCML.

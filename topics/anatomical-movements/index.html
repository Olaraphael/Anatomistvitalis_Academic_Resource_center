Anatomical movements and joint terminology

Movement terms describe how one body part changes position relative to another. They are most precise when the joint, starting position and plane are specified. Many movements are combinations rather than pure single-plane actions, and their range depends on joint structure, soft tissues, muscle activity and individual variation.

Learning objectives

High-yield study points

  • Flexion generally decreases the angle between segments; extension generally increases it. Hyperextension means extension beyond the usual anatomical range, where that term is appropriate.
  • Abduction moves a part away from the median plane; adduction moves it toward the median plane. For digits, the reference axis differs by region: the hand uses the third digit and the foot commonly uses the second digit.
  • Medial (internal) rotation turns the anterior surface toward the median plane; lateral (external) rotation turns it away.
  • Circumduction combines flexion, extension, abduction and adduction so the distal end traces a circle or cone.
  • Pronation and supination describe forearm rotation: in anatomical position the palm faces anteriorly; pronation turns it posteriorly, while supination returns it anteriorly. These terms also have specialised meanings in the foot.
  • Dorsiflexion brings the dorsum of the foot toward the leg; plantarflexion points the foot downward. Inversion turns the sole medially; eversion turns it laterally.
  • Elevation and depression raise and lower a structure; protraction and retraction move it anteriorly and posteriorly; opposition brings the thumb toward a fingertip.
  • Joint movement should be described at the relevant joint: flexion at the elbow differs from flexion at the shoulder in its direction and functional effect.

Clinical connection

Movement vocabulary is essential in musculoskeletal examination and rehabilitation. Record pain, active versus passive range, side, and the specific joint rather than using a generic phrase such as 'the limb moves poorly'.

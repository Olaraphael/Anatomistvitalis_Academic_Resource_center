The cardiac cycle

The cardiac cycle is the sequence of electrical and mechanical events occurring during one heartbeat. Atrial and ventricular systole alternate with relaxation. Pressure gradients determine valve opening and closure.

Explore lessons and quizzesBrowse all topic pages

Learning objectives

  • Describe the intrinsic cardiac conduction pathway from the sinoatrial node through the His–Purkinje system.
  • Relate depolarisation and repolarisation to the main ECG waves.
  • Explain how autonomic input modifies heart rate and conduction.
  • Distinguish electrical activation from the mechanical events of the cardiac cycle.

High-yield study points

  • Ventricular filling occurs mainly while atrioventricular valves are open.
  • Atrial systole contributes to final ventricular filling.
  • Isovolumetric contraction begins after atrioventricular valves close and before semilunar valves open.
  • Ventricular ejection occurs when ventricular pressure exceeds pressure in the aorta or pulmonary trunk.
  • Isovolumetric relaxation follows semilunar valve closure before filling resumes.

Clinical connection

Heart sounds are associated with valve closure and surrounding haemodynamic events; interpretation requires clinical context.

Check your understanding

  1. Which structure normally initiates each heartbeat?
    Reveal answer

    The sinoatrial node.

  2. What is the main significance of the AV nodal delay?
    Reveal answer

    It allows time for atrial contraction and ventricular filling before ventricular activation.

  3. What does the T wave represent on a standard ECG?
    Reveal answer

    Ventricular repolarisation.

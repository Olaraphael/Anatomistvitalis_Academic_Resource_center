Week 2: implantation and the bilaminar embryonic disc

18 min studyFree learning resourceIncludes memory aid

Lesson overview

Implantation progresses as trophoblast differentiates into cytotrophoblast and syncytiotrophoblast. The syncytiotrophoblast invades the endometrium and contributes to early maternal-fetal exchange. The embryoblast forms a bilaminar disc of epiblast and hypoblast. The amniotic cavity develops adjacent to the epiblast, while the yolk sac develops in relation to the hypoblast. Extraembryonic mesoderm and the chorionic cavity help organise the early embryo and its supporting tissues.

Learning objectives

  • Describe trophoblast differentiation.
  • Identify epiblast, hypoblast, amniotic cavity and yolk sac.
  • Explain why week 2 is called the bilaminar-disc stage.

High-yield points

  • Cytotrophoblast is the inner cellular layer; syncytiotrophoblast is the outer invasive layer.
  • Epiblast gives rise to all three definitive germ layers during gastrulation.
  • Hypoblast contributes to extraembryonic lining rather than the definitive germ layers of the embryo proper.
  • Implantation is a coordinated interaction with receptive endometrium.

Memory aid / mnemonic

Week 2 = “Two layers, two spaces”: epiblast + hypoblast; amniotic cavity + yolk sac. Trophoblast: cyto + syncytio.

Study tip: hide this section, explain the concept aloud, then check whether you remembered the sequence and the reason behind it.

Clinical connection

Ectopic implantation can be dangerous. Pain or bleeding in early pregnancy requires appropriate clinical assessment.

Week two: organise the two-layer disc

The embryoblast differentiates into epiblast and hypoblast, creating the bilaminar embryonic disc. The epiblast faces the amniotic cavity and later supplies the three definitive germ layers of the embryo. Hypoblast cells contribute to lining of the yolk sac and extraembryonic structures; they do not form the definitive endoderm of the embryo proper.

The trophoblast differentiates into cytotrophoblast, a cellular layer, and syncytiotrophoblast, a multinucleated invasive layer. The syncytiotrophoblast expands into the endometrium and begins to establish spaces involved in early maternal blood exchange. Extraembryonic mesoderm and the chorionic cavity develop during this period.

Why the two-layer distinction matters

The bilaminar disc is a temporary organisation. During week three, epiblast cells ingress through the primitive streak: some displace the hypoblast to form definitive endoderm, others form mesoderm, and the epiblast remaining at the surface becomes ectoderm. This is gastrulation.

Structures to identify in a diagram

  • Amniotic cavity above the epiblast.
  • Epiblast and hypoblast forming the bilaminar disc.
  • Yolk sac associated with hypoblast-derived lining.
  • Cytotrophoblast and syncytiotrophoblast surrounding the conceptus.
  • Chorionic cavity and extraembryonic mesoderm as development progresses.

When interpreting a diagram, orient it first, label the cavities, then identify the two disc layers and outer trophoblastic layers. Avoid memorising isolated labels without spatial relationships.

Quick self-test

  1. Which layer ultimately gives rise to the three germ layers of the embryo proper?
  2. Which trophoblastic layer is multinucleated and invasive?
  3. Does hypoblast form the definitive endoderm of the embryo proper?
Reveal answers and explanations

1. Epiblast. 2. Syncytiotrophoblast. 3. No. Definitive endoderm is formed by epiblast cells during gastrulation.

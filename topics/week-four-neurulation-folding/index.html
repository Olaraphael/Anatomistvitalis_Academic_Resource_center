Week 4: neurulation, body folding and neural tube closure

22 min studyFree learning resourceIncludes memory aid

Lesson overview

Signals from the notochord and nearby mesoderm induce ectoderm to form the neural plate. The plate bends into a neural groove; neural folds approach and fuse to form the neural tube. Cranial and caudal neuropores close during the fourth post-fertilisation week. Neural crest cells detach from the folds and migrate to form many peripheral nervous system structures and other derivatives. Cranio-caudal and lateral folding reshape the flat disc, incorporate endoderm into the primitive gut tube and reposition developing structures.

Learning objectives

  • Trace neural plate, groove, folds and tube.
  • Explain the role of neural crest cells.
  • Describe body folding and primitive gut formation.

High-yield points

  • Neural tube forms the brain and spinal cord.
  • Neural crest contributes to sensory and autonomic ganglia, Schwann cells, melanocytes and several craniofacial tissues.
  • Neural tube closure occurs progressively along the body axis.
  • Folding converts the flat embryonic disc into a more cylindrical body form.

Memory aid / mnemonic

Neurulation: “Plate → Groove → Folds → Tube.” Neural crest = travelling edge cells that migrate widely.

Study tip: hide this section, explain the concept aloud, then check whether you remembered the sequence and its rationale.

Clinical connection

Failure of neural tube closure can cause neural tube defects. Periconceptional folate reduces risk but does not prevent every case; follow qualified local clinical guidance.

Neurulation: from neural plate to neural tube

Signals from axial mesoderm, including the notochord, help induce the overlying ectoderm to become neural plate. The plate bends to form a neural groove bordered by neural folds. The folds approach and fuse, producing the neural tube, which becomes the brain and spinal cord. Closure progresses along the axis and the cranial and caudal neuropores close at different times during the fourth week.

Cells at the neural plate border form neural crest, which separates and migrates to many destinations. Neural crest derivatives include sensory and autonomic ganglia, Schwann cells, melanocytes, adrenal medulla and important craniofacial components. The neural tube and neural crest have related origins but distinct major derivative patterns.

Folding creates body form

Cranio-caudal and lateral folding transform the flat embryonic disc into a more cylindrical body. Folding incorporates part of the yolk sac into the primitive gut tube, brings the heart region to a more ventral position and helps close the ventral body wall. It also changes the spatial relationships between structures, so early positions cannot always be interpreted like adult anatomy.

Clinical connection

Failure of neural tube closure can produce neural tube defects, whose type and severity depend on the site and extent of the defect. Neural tube development is multifactorial; periconceptional folic acid reduces risk but does not prevent every case. Prenatal care and individual risk assessment should be guided by qualified clinicians.

Quick self-test

  1. What adult structures arise from the neural tube?
  2. Name two neural crest derivatives.
  3. What does embryonic folding help incorporate into the body?
Reveal answers and explanations

1. Brain and spinal cord. 2. Examples include sensory ganglia, autonomic ganglia, Schwann cells, melanocytes or adrenal medulla. 3. Part of the yolk sac into the primitive gut tube.

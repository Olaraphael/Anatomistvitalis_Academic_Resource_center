The Anatomistvitalis Academic Curriculum

Study the subjects in a deliberate sequence. Each subject has its own home; related lessons are linked where useful, but anatomy, physiology, pathology and rehabilitation remain distinct disciplines. Begin at Stage 1 if you are new to the subject, or enter at the stage that matches your current course and prerequisites.

STAGE 1

History and Foundations of Anatomy

Learn the language, orientation and scope of anatomy before moving into specialised branches.

  • History, scope and branches of human anatomy
  • Anatomical terminology and standard anatomical position
  • Planes, axes, sections and orientation
  • Directional terms and body regions
  • Body divisions, cavities and serous membranes
  • Anatomical movements and joint terminology
  • Levels of organisation and anatomical variation
  • Roots, abbreviations, eponyms and nomenclature
  • Gross anatomy: systemic, regional, surface and applied approaches
STAGE 2

Developmental Anatomy (Embryology)

Follow human development from gamete formation and fertilisation to organ formation, fetal development and congenital anomalies.

  • History, scope and introduction to embryology
  • Embryology: fertilisation, implantation and early development
  • Gametogenesis and fertilisation
  • Week one: cleavage and blastocyst formation
  • Week two: implantation and bilaminar disc
  • Week three: gastrulation and notochord
  • Week four: neurulation and embryonic folding
  • Weeks five to eight: organogenesis
  • Germ layers, organogenesis and congenital anomalies
  • Fetal period, placenta and fetal circulation
  • Placenta, pregnancy and fetal development
  • Congenital anomalies and teratology
STAGE 3

Histology and Cell Biology

Understand cells, tissues and the microscopic organisation of organs before interpreting organ function or disease.

  • Introduction to microscopic anatomy and histological methods
  • Cell membrane and transport
  • Cell structure and organelles
  • The four basic tissue classes
  • Epithelial tissue
  • Epithelial tissue classification and recognition
  • Connective tissue and extracellular matrix
  • Cartilage, tendons, ligaments and bone histology
  • Skeletal, cardiac and smooth muscle tissue
  • Respiratory histology and the blood–air barrier
  • Renal histology: nephron and glomerulus
STAGE 4

Gross Anatomy: Systemic and Regional

Study structures visible to the unaided eye. Learn bones, joints, muscles, nerves, vessels, organs, relationships and clinically important spaces.

General, skeletal and musculoskeletal anatomy

  • Skeletal system overview
  • Bone classification and landmarks
  • Bone tissue and remodelling
  • Joint classification
  • Major synovial joints
  • Vertebral column, discs and ligaments
  • Skeletal muscle attachments, actions and compartments
  • Bone remodelling and fracture healing
  • Skin, hair, nails and glands

Regional anatomy: learn in this order

  • Back and vertebral column
  • Upper limb
  • Shoulder region and joint
  • Hand: tendons, nerves and intrinsic muscles
  • Lower limb
  • Foot and ankle
  • Thorax and abdomen overview
  • Thoracic cavity, pleura and mediastinum
  • Abdominal wall, inguinal canal and hernias
  • Abdominal quadrants, nine regions and surface lines
  • Head anatomy overview
  • Bones of the skull
  • Head and neck
  • Pelvis, pelvic floor and perineum

Systemic gross anatomy

  • Heart and great vessels
  • Respiratory tract and lungs
  • Digestive tract and accessory organs
  • Kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra
  • Male and female reproductive anatomy
  • Endocrine glands
  • Lymphatic vessels, nodes, spleen and thymus
  • Eye, ear, smell and taste anatomy
STAGE 5

Neuroanatomy

Study the organisation of the nervous system, then localise pathways and lesions.

  • Organisation of the nervous system
  • Neuroanatomy foundations
  • Neuron and resting membrane potential (cross-linked with physiology)
  • Spinal cord, segments, roots and spinal nerves
  • Meninges and cerebrospinal fluid
  • Cerebral lobes and functions
  • The twelve cranial nerves
  • Brainstem and cranial nerve nuclei
  • Ascending sensory and descending motor pathways
  • Advanced ascending sensory pathways
  • Advanced descending motor pathways
  • Cerebellum and basal ganglia
  • Limbic system and memory
  • Ventricles, CSF and hydrocephalus
  • Cerebral blood supply and circle of Willis
  • Neurovascular anatomy and stroke localisation
  • Spinal cord tracts and lesion localisation
STAGE 6

Surface, Radiological and Clinical Anatomy

Apply structural knowledge to examination, imaging and clinical reasoning.

  • Surface landmarks and abdominal regions
  • X-ray, CT, MRI and ultrasound anatomy
  • Applying anatomy to symptoms and examination
  • Cardiac conduction system and ECG orientation
STAGE 7

Physiology: Normal Function of the Body

Physiology is taught as a full course with distinct branches and system-based modules. Follow the Physiology Course Map for the recommended sequence, mechanisms, integrated learning and self-checks. Start with homeostasis, then study blood and body fluids, neurophysiology, muscle, cardiovascular, respiratory, renal, gastrointestinal, endocrine and reproductive physiology.

  • Complete Physiology Course Map: all branches and study sequence
  • Blood physiology: plasma and formed elements
  • Haemostasis, coagulation and fibrinolysis
  • General physiology, homeostasis and feedback
  • Homeostasis and feedback
  • Cardiovascular physiology
  • The cardiac cycle
  • Cardiac output, blood pressure and vascular resistance
  • Respiratory physiology
  • Respiratory mechanics and lung volumes
  • Ventilation–perfusion matching
  • Renal physiology
  • Glomerular filtration and renal clearance
  • Endocrine physiology
  • Thyroid and adrenal feedback axes
  • Digestive physiology
  • Digestion and absorption
  • Muscle excitation, contraction and force
  • Autonomic physiology
  • Reproductive physiology and hormonal cycles
  • Fluid, electrolyte and acid–base balance
STAGE 8

Pathology: Mechanisms of Disease

Learn how normal cells, tissues and organ systems become diseased.

  • General pathology: cell injury, inflammation, healing, haemodynamic disorders and neoplasia
STAGE 9

Physiotherapy and Rehabilitation

Apply anatomy, physiology and pathology to movement, function, recovery and participation.

  • Physiotherapy: scope and professional foundations
  • Physiotherapy assessment and clinical reasoning
  • Therapeutic exercise
  • Musculoskeletal rehabilitation
  • Neurological rehabilitation
  • Cardiorespiratory physiotherapy

Supporting Biomedical Sciences

Use these subjects to explain molecular mechanisms and the context of health and disease.

  • Biochemistry and molecular biology
  • Metabolism, ATP and energy balance
  • Human genetics and molecular medicine
  • Blood and immune function
  • Blood components and plasma
  • Innate and adaptive immunity
  • Haemostasis, coagulation and fibrinolysis
  • Integumentary system

Research Methodology and Academic Writing

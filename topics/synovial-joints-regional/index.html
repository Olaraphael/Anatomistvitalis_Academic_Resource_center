Major synovial joints: shoulder, hip, knee and elbow

Synovial joints share a cavity and capsule but differ in shape and mechanical demands. The shoulder (glenohumeral joint) sacrifices some bony stability for a large range of motion; the rotator cuff, labrum, capsule and scapular muscles contribute to control. The hip is a deep ball-and-socket joint with strong capsular ligaments and a deep acetabulum. The knee is a modified hinge joint with menisci, cruciate and collateral ligaments. The elbow complex combines hinge-like flexion-extension with proximal radioulnar rotation for pronation and supination.

Learning objectives

  • Compare the shoulder and hip in mobility and stability.
  • Identify major stabilisers of the knee.
  • Explain how the elbow permits flexion-extension and forearm rotation.

High-yield study points

  • The glenoid labrum deepens the shoulder socket; rotator cuff muscles dynamically stabilise the humeral head.
  • The acetabular labrum deepens the hip socket and helps maintain joint congruence.
  • The anterior cruciate ligament resists anterior translation of the tibia relative to the femur and contributes to rotational control.
  • The medial and lateral collateral ligaments resist selected valgus and varus stresses.
  • The annular ligament holds the radial head against the ulna during forearm rotation.

Clinical connection

Shoulder dislocation, anterior cruciate ligament injury, hip fracture and elbow dislocation affect different stabilising structures. Clinical tests must be interpreted alongside history and imaging when indicated.

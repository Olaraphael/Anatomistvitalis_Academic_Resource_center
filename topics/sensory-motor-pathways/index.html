Ascending sensory and descending motor pathways

Long tracts connect peripheral receptors, spinal cord, brainstem, thalamus and cerebral cortex. The dorsal column–medial lemniscus pathway carries discriminative touch, vibration and conscious proprioception; its primary fibres ascend ipsilaterally in the spinal cord and cross in the medulla. The anterolateral system carries pain, temperature and aspects of crude touch, with fibres commonly crossing near their spinal entry level. Corticospinal pathways contribute to skilled voluntary movement; many fibres cross at the pyramidal decussation in the caudal medulla. Lesion patterns depend on the level and extent of injury.

Learning objectives

  • Compare the dorsal column and anterolateral sensory pathways.
  • Describe the broad course of the corticospinal tract.
  • Explain why lesion level affects sensory and motor findings.

High-yield study points

  • Dorsal column fibres ascend ipsilaterally before synapsing in the gracile or cuneate nuclei of the medulla.
  • Anterolateral pathway fibres typically cross within a few spinal segments of entry.
  • The lateral corticospinal tract descends after most fibres cross at the pyramidal decussation.
  • Upper motor neuron lesions may produce weakness, increased tone and brisk reflexes; lower motor neuron lesions may cause weakness, reduced tone and reduced reflexes.
  • Real neurological examinations show combinations of findings; no single sign should be interpreted in isolation.

Clinical connection

A spinal cord lesion can affect pathways differently according to its level and side. New weakness, numbness, gait change or loss of bladder/bowel control needs prompt medical assessment.

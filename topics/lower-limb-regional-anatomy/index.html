Regional anatomy of the lower limb

The lower limb includes the gluteal region, hip, thigh, knee, leg, ankle and foot. The femur is the principal thigh bone; the tibia bears most weight in the leg, while the fibula provides muscle attachment and contributes to ankle stability. The femoral triangle contains the femoral nerve, artery and vein in a clinically important arrangement. The sciatic nerve usually divides into tibial and common fibular components. Anterior leg muscles dorsiflex the ankle, lateral muscles evert the foot, and posterior muscles plantarflex; the foot's arches distribute load and assist locomotion.

Learning objectives

  • Identify the major bones and regions of the lower limb.
  • Summarise the femoral triangle and major nerve pathways.
  • Explain the functional organisation of leg compartments and foot arches.

High-yield study points

  • The femoral nerve is lateral to the femoral artery in the femoral triangle; the femoral vein is medial.
  • The obturator nerve mainly supplies the medial thigh compartment.
  • The common fibular nerve is vulnerable near the fibular neck; injury can cause foot drop.
  • The tibial nerve passes through the tarsal tunnel behind the medial malleolus.
  • The medial longitudinal, lateral longitudinal and transverse arches help distribute body weight.

Clinical connection

Hip fractures, knee ligament injuries, compartment syndrome and peripheral nerve lesions can impair gait. Neurovascular examination and appropriate imaging help guide clinical evaluation.

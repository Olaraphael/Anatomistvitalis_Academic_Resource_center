Anatomy of endocrine glands

The endocrine system comprises hormone-secreting cells and glands distributed throughout the body. The hypothalamus and pituitary form a major neuroendocrine control centre; the thyroid lies in the anterior neck, parathyroids are usually posterior to it, adrenal glands sit superior to the kidneys, and pancreatic islets are embedded within the pancreas. Gonads also have endocrine functions. Endocrine glands are highly vascular, allowing hormones to enter circulation. Their structure ranges from discrete encapsulated glands to scattered endocrine cells in organs such as the gastrointestinal tract.

Learning objectives

  • Locate the major endocrine glands.
  • Distinguish endocrine glands from exocrine glands.
  • Connect gland location with broad hormonal function.

High-yield study points

  • The pituitary lies in the sella turcica and is connected to the hypothalamus by the infundibulum.
  • The thyroid has right and left lobes joined by an isthmus in many individuals.
  • Adrenal cortex and medulla have distinct embryological origins and hormone products.
  • The endocrine pancreas contains islets with several hormone-secreting cell types.
  • Endocrine tissues generally release hormones into interstitial fluid and then blood rather than through ducts.

Clinical connection

Thyroid enlargement, pituitary masses and adrenal disorders can produce local effects or systemic hormonal changes. Diagnosis relies on clinical assessment, laboratory testing and sometimes imaging.

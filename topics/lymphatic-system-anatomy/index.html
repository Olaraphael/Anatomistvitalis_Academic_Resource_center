Lymphatic vessels, lymph nodes, spleen and thymus

The lymphatic system returns excess interstitial fluid and proteins to the bloodstream, transports absorbed dietary lipids from intestinal lacteals and supports immune surveillance. Lymphatic capillaries collect fluid into vessels that pass through lymph nodes before returning lymph to venous circulation. The right lymphatic duct drains much of the right upper quadrant of the body; the thoracic duct drains most of the remainder, with anatomical variation. Lymph nodes filter lymph, the spleen filters blood and supports immune responses to blood-borne antigens, and the thymus supports T-cell maturation, especially early in life.

Learning objectives

  • Explain the major functions of lymphatic circulation.
  • Distinguish lymph nodes, spleen and thymus.
  • Describe the broad drainage territories of the main lymphatic ducts.

High-yield study points

  • Lymph flow depends on valves, skeletal-muscle activity, respiratory pressure changes and smooth muscle in larger lymphatic vessels.
  • Afferent lymphatic vessels enter most lymph nodes; efferent vessels leave at the hilum.
  • The spleen filters blood rather than lymph and has red-pulp and white-pulp functions.
  • The thymus is a primary lymphoid organ where T lymphocytes mature.
  • Lymphatic drainage patterns are clinically important but can vary and overlap.

Clinical connection

Lymph-node enlargement can occur with infection, inflammation or malignancy; its meaning depends on distribution, duration and other findings. Persistent or unexplained enlargement should be assessed clinically.

Check your understanding: lymphatic anatomy

  1. What is the main difference between lymphatic capillaries and blood capillaries?
    Answer

    Lymphatic capillaries are blind-ended, highly permeable vessels specialised to collect interstitial fluid, proteins and other material; their overlapping endothelial flaps act as one-way entry points.

  2. Where does lymph from most of the body return to the venous circulation?
    Answer

    Through the thoracic duct into the left venous angle (junction of the left internal jugular and subclavian veins). The right lymphatic duct drains the right upper quadrant; anatomical variations occur.

  3. How does the spleen differ from a lymph node in what it filters?
    Answer

    The spleen filters blood and responds to blood-borne antigens; lymph nodes filter lymph arriving from tissues.

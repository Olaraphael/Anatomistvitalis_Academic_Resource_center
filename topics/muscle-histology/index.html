Muscle tissue: skeletal, cardiac and smooth

Muscle tissue generates force through organised contractile proteins. Skeletal muscle fibres are long, cylindrical, multinucleated cells with prominent striations and are usually under voluntary somatic control. Cardiac muscle cells are striated, branched and connected by intercalated discs containing mechanical and electrical junctions. Smooth muscle cells are spindle-shaped, lack visible striations and are controlled mainly by autonomic, hormonal and local signals. Skeletal muscle is arranged into fibres, fascicles and whole muscles, supported by connective tissue layers. Cardiac muscle forms the myocardium and must coordinate contraction to pump blood. Smooth muscle occurs in vessel walls and hollow organs such as the intestine, airways, bladder and uterus. Each type has different patterns of calcium regulation, contraction speed, fatigue resistance and control. On histology, examine striations, cell shape, nuclear position, branching and intercellular junctions. No single feature should be interpreted without the overall tissue context.

Learning objectives

  • Identify the defining microscopic features of the three muscle types.
  • Relate muscle structure to control and function.
  • Describe the organisational hierarchy of skeletal muscle.

High-yield study points

  • Skeletal muscle: striated, long fibres, multiple peripheral nuclei.
  • Cardiac muscle: striated, branched cells, central nuclei and intercalated discs.
  • Smooth muscle: non-striated spindle-shaped cells with central nuclei.
  • Skeletal muscle supports movement and posture; cardiac muscle pumps blood; smooth muscle regulates hollow organs and vessel diameter.
  • Muscle contraction depends on actin–myosin interactions and calcium regulation.

Clinical connection

Muscle biopsies and laboratory tests may help evaluate selected neuromuscular diseases, but interpretation requires specialist pathology and clinical information.

Check your understanding

  1. Which muscle type is striated and usually under voluntary control?
    Reveal answer

    Skeletal muscle.

  2. Which muscle type contains intercalated discs?
    Reveal answer

    Cardiac muscle.

  3. How can smooth muscle be distinguished from skeletal muscle in a typical histological section?
    Reveal answer

    Smooth muscle cells are generally spindle-shaped, have central nuclei and lack the conspicuous cross-striations of skeletal muscle.

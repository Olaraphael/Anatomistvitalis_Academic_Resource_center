Pathology — disease mechanisms and tissue change

A structured introduction to mechanisms of disease, cellular injury, inflammation, healing, haemodynamic disorders, neoplasia and organ-system pathology.

Recommended learning order

Study normal anatomy, histology and physiology before pathology. Learn general disease mechanisms first, then compare how those mechanisms appear in specific organs.

1. Foundations of pathologyDisease definitions, aetiology, pathogenesis, morphology and clinicopathological reasoning. 2. Cell structure and normal functionA reference point for understanding reversible injury and cell death. 3. Normal tissue architectureReview baseline tissue structure before interpreting abnormal morphology. 4. Congenital anomalies and teratologyDevelopmental mechanisms, risk, timing and evidence appraisal. 5. Reading pathology evidenceAssess study design, methods, uncertainty and the limits of causal claims.

Visual model: disease process

Core topics to develop in sequence

  1. Cell adaptation, injury, reversible injury and cell death.
  2. Acute and chronic inflammation; chemical mediators and outcomes.
  3. Repair, regeneration, fibrosis and wound healing.
  4. Oedema, thrombosis, embolism, infarction and shock.
  5. Immune-mediated disease and infection-related tissue injury.
  6. Neoplasia: nomenclature, differentiation, invasion, metastasis and grading/staging.
  7. Systemic pathology linked to organ histology and physiology.

Pathology images should include a credible source, tissue or organ, stain when known, magnification or scale when available, and an explanation of the finding. A photomicrograph should not be labelled with a diagnosis that the image alone cannot establish.

Real histopathology example: renal glomerulus

When interpreting a micrograph, first identify the organ and compartment, then describe what is actually visible, compare it with normal architecture, and only then consider a possible pathological interpretation. Do not infer a specific cause from a single image alone.

Study standard: use the article explanations alongside labelled visuals, self-test questions and cited further reading.

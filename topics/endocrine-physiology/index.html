Endocrine physiology: hormones and feedback loops

The endocrine system coordinates processes through hormones released into the circulation or local extracellular environment. Hormone action depends on how it is synthesised, transported, recognised by receptors and cleared. Hormonal effects are shaped by receptor distribution, signal pathways, pulsatile secretion, circadian patterns and feedback from the regulated variable.

High-yield study points

  • Peptide and many protein hormones bind cell-surface receptors; steroid hormones and thyroid hormones act largely through intracellular receptors, although signalling is more varied than this simple grouping suggests.
  • The hypothalamus and pituitary coordinate multiple endocrine axes, while other glands such as thyroid, parathyroids, adrenals, pancreas and gonads have distinct roles.
  • Negative feedback from target-gland hormones commonly regulates hypothalamic and pituitary output.
  • Insulin promotes nutrient storage and lowers blood glucose in many contexts; glucagon helps mobilise fuel during fasting, with effects depending on the organ and physiological state.
  • Cortisol supports stress adaptation and metabolism; aldosterone contributes to sodium, potassium and volume regulation; catecholamines mediate rapid responses.
  • Hormones may circulate bound to carrier proteins, which influence half-life and the free fraction available to tissues.
  • Hormone levels vary with time, stress, meals, sleep, reproductive stage and medications; one laboratory value must be interpreted in context.

Clinical connection

Endocrine symptoms often develop across multiple systems because hormones act on distant targets. Diagnosis usually combines clinical features with appropriately timed and interpreted laboratory tests.

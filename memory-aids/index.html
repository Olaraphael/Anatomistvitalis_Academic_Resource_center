ACTIVE RECALL TOOLKIT

Memory aids and anatomy mnemonics

Mnemonics help you retrieve key facts. They work best when you understand the anatomy first, practise recalling it without looking, and then check the underlying explanation. Some mnemonics vary between textbooks; use the version taught by your course where it differs.

CRANIAL NERVES

Sensory / motor / both

“Some Say Marry Money, But My Brother Says Big Brains Matter More.”

Sequence for CN I–XII: S, S, M, M, B, M, B, S, B, B, M, M. This is a first-pass cue; learn each nerve’s specific functions separately.

Review cranial nerves ↗

MENINGES

Coverings of brain and spinal cord

Dura → Arachnoid → Pia (“DAP”).

CSF is in the subarachnoid space, between arachnoid and pia.

Review meninges and CSF ↗

SENSORY PATHWAYS

Where do pathways cross?

DCML crosses in the medulla; pain and temperature cross in the spinal cord.

Always connect modality, crossing point and side of deficit.

Study ascending pathways ↗

MOTOR PATHWAYS

Upper and lower motor neuron patterns

UMN: often increased tone and reflexes. LMN: reduced tone and reflexes, atrophy and fasciculations.

Acute lesions and specific disorders can produce exceptions.

Study motor pathways ↗

EMBRYOLOGY

The early weeks

1 Divide · 2 Two layers · 3 Three layers · 4 Fold and close · 5–8 Build organ plans.

Use post-fertilisation age. Gestational age is usually about two weeks greater.

Start embryology ↗

GERM LAYERS

Broad derivatives

Ectoderm: epidermis and nervous system. Mesoderm: muscle, bone, vessels. Endoderm: gut and respiratory epithelial linings.

Many organs contain derivatives from more than one layer.

Study gastrulation ↗

NEURULATION

Neural tube formation

Plate → Groove → Folds → Tube.

Neural tube forms CNS; neural crest contributes to many PNS structures and other derivatives.

Study neurulation ↗

FETAL CIRCULATION

Three important shunts

D-F-D: Ductus venosus, Foramen ovale, Ductus arteriosus.

Recall what each shunt bypasses, not just the name.

Study fetal circulation ↗

SPINAL NERVES

Dorsal and ventral roots

DAVE: Dorsal Afferent, Ventral Efferent.

Dorsal roots carry sensory input; ventral roots carry motor output.

Review spinal cord anatomy ↗

FETAL AGE

Age distinction

Gestational age ≈ post-fertilisation age + 2 weeks.

This is an approximate convention; individual dating depends on clinical context.

Review developmental timing ↗

CEREBRAL ARTERIES

Broad vascular territories

ACA = leg area; MCA = lateral face/arm and dominant-side language; PCA = visual cortex.

These are learning anchors, not diagnostic rules.

Study stroke localisation ↗

CSF

Ventricular flow

Lateral → Third → Aqueduct → Fourth → Out.

Flow proceeds to the subarachnoid space and then into venous drainage pathways.

Study ventricles and CSF ↗

BRAINSTEM

Cranial nerve level groups

CN III–IV: midbrain · V–VIII: pons · IX–XII: medulla.

A broad orientation cue. CN XI also has spinal roots; learn individual nuclei and exit points separately.

Review brainstem nuclei ↗

NEURAL CREST

Selected neural crest derivatives

“C-SAMe”: Craniofacial structures, Schwann cells, Adrenal medulla, Melanocytes, sensory/autonomic ganglia.

Selected high-yield examples, not a complete list. Some structures have mixed embryological origins.

Review neurulation ↗

DEVELOPMENTAL TIMING

Key early milestones

6–7 implants · 3 layers · 4 closes · 5–8 organ plans · 9+ fetal growth.

Approximate post-fertilisation timing: implantation begins around days 6–7, gastrulation in week 3, neural tube closure in week 4.

Review the week-by-week course ↗

CORTICAL LOCALISATION

Motor, sensory and vision

Precentral = motor; postcentral = somatic sensation; occipital = vision.

Functional areas overlap and vary; use this as a map, then study the relevant pathways and vascular territories.

Review brain anatomy ↗

NEUROANATOMY

Trace a pathway in order

Origin → tract → crossing → relay → cortex or effector.

For a lesion question, ask what is carried, where fibres cross and whether the finding is above or below that crossing.

Practise pathway localisation ↗

How to use these: read the cue, cover it, reproduce the sequence from memory, explain why it is true, then answer a question or draw the pathway. If a mnemonic encourages a wrong simplification, prioritise the anatomical explanation.
